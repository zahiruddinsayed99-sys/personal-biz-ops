### Step B — Calculate the opening range

For each selected date, inspect these five candles:

| Candle | Time      |
| ------ | --------- |
| 1      | 9:15–9:16 |
| 2      | 9:16–9:17 |
| 3      | 9:17–9:18 |
| 4      | 9:18–9:19 |
| 5      | 9:19–9:20 |

Record:

- Range High: highest high across all five candles.
- Range Low: lowest low across all five candles.

Use the candle's actual high and low, including wicks. Draw two horizontal lines at those prices.

### Step C — Identify the breakout

Starting with the candle opening at 9:20 AM, inspect each 1-minute candle in sequence.

- If its close is above the Range High, record a bullish breakout.
- If its close is below the Range Low, record a bearish breakdown.
- If neither occurs, keep checking until the entry deadline.

A wick crossing a boundary without the candle closing beyond it does not count.

### Step D — Wait for the retest

For a bullish setup, look for price to return to the Range High and then produce a bullish-direction 1-minute close.

For a bearish setup, look for price to return to the Range Low and then produce a bearish-direction 1-minute close.

Record the confirmation time. For a manual backtest, use the next available price after the confirmation candle closes as the entry assumption; do not assume you could have entered at the confirmation candle's closing price before it was known.

### Step E — Record stop-loss and outcome

For a bullish setup, the underlying stop is the Range Low. For a bearish setup, it is the Range High.

Record whether the stop was reached after entry and the time it happened. Also record the maximum favorable movement before the stop or exit.

We still need a fixed profit-taking and time-based exit rule before calculating comparable win rates and returns. Until then, record the price movement without labeling a trade a winner or loser based on hindsight.
