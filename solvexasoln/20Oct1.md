# SwingPilot — SENSEX Early Sweep Detector

Prototype V1.0 · Manual validation in Zerodha Kite · India market hours (IST)

The objective is to detect a potential reversal soon after a liquidity sweep, rather than waiting for a large reversal candle or using a conventional opening-range breakout. We’ll use a balanced entry: sweep → reclaim → 1-minute micro-structure break → entry.

The prototype will be designed for early signals, but it will not assume that a sweep guarantees a reversal or that the strategy is profitable. We’ll first make the rules mechanical, then test them candle by candle using Zerodha Kite charts.

## 2. Exact prototype rules

These are proposed test parameters, not established market edges.

### A. Chart setup in Zerodha Kite

| Setting                 | Rule                                                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Instrument              | SENSEX index chart                                                       |
| Context timeframe       | 5 minutes                                                                |
| Entry timeframe         | 1 minute                                                                 |
| Indicators              | ATR(14) on 5-minute chart                                                |
| Trading window          | 9:30–11:00 AM IST                                                        |
| Maximum trades          | 1 per trading day                                                        |
| Intended holding period | 15–60 minutes                                                            |
| Expiry/options          | Validate the index signal first; evaluate the tradable option separately |

Use the same SENSEX instrument and candle settings throughout the test. Do not switch between index and futures candles mid-test.

### B. Liquidity levels to mark before trading

Mark these levels before the session or as they become objectively available:

1. Previous trading day's high and low.
2. Opening range high and low, using the 9:15–9:30 AM period.
3. Confirmed 5-minute swing highs and lows formed during the session.

A swing high is a candle whose high exceeds the highs of the two candles before it and the two candles after it. A swing low is defined in the reverse way. A swing is only usable after the two candles to its right have closed—otherwise the test would use information that was not yet available.

If several levels are close together, treat them as one zone for this prototype. Use the outermost boundary of the zone as the reference and record the choice before seeing the outcome.

### C. Sweep detection — the first event

Use the 5-minute ATR(14) to scale the sweep threshold to the current market's volatility.

Let:

- \\(A\\) = latest completed 5-minute ATR(14)
- \\(L\\) = marked liquidity level
- \\(H\\), \\(Lo\\), \\(C\\) = the current 5-minute candle's high, low and close

A sweep must happen after the marked level exists and is known to the trader.

| Rule                 | Bullish sweep                   | Bearish sweep                   |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Level crossed        | Candle low is below \\(L\\)     | Candle high is above \\(L\\)    |
| Minimum penetration  | \\(L-Lo \ge 0.05A\\)            | \\(H-L \ge 0.05A\\)             |
| Reclaim confirmation | 5-minute close is above \\(L\\) | 5-minute close is below \\(L\\) |
| Event deadline       | Within the same 5-minute candle | Within the same 5-minute candle |

Balanced entry improvement: start watching the 1-minute chart as soon as price crosses the level. But do not enter merely because the 1-minute price briefly reclaims it. The 5-minute close-back-inside rule is the primary confirmation in V1.

This avoids treating every touch of a level as a sweep. It does mean that some very fast reversals will be missed; we should measure that trade-off rather than remove confirmation without evidence.

### D. Reclaim and 1-minute structure break

After the 5-minute sweep candle closes:

1. Switch to the 1-minute chart.
2. Identify the most recent confirmed 1-minute swing point formed before the structure-break candle.
3. Wait for a 1-minute candle to close beyond that swing in the reversal direction.
4. Enter at the next available executable price, not retrospectively at the breakout candle's close.

A 1-minute swing uses the same two-candles-left, two-candles-right definition. It must be confirmed before the breakout candle closes.

Bullish example — illustrative prices only

1. Marked low: 80,000.
2. 5-minute candle trades to 79,960 and closes at 80,025: a reclaim, assuming the ATR threshold is met.
3. On the 1-minute chart, the latest confirmed swing high is 80,060.
4. A 1-minute candle closes at 80,075: bullish structure break.
5. Enter on the next available price, subject to stop and target checks.

The prices are hypothetical and are not a live SENSEX signal.

For bearish setups, reverse the logic: sweep above a marked high, close back below it, then close below the latest confirmed 1-minute swing low.

No retest is required in V1. Requiring a retest may improve entry quality but can delay entry or cause missed trades. We'll record whether a retest happened so we can compare it later without changing the original test rules.

### E. Stop-loss, target and trade rejection

| Item                | Exact V1 rule                                                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bullish stop        | Sweep low minus \\(0.05A\\)                                   |
| Bearish stop        | Sweep high plus \\(0.05A\\)                                   |
| Risk per unit       | Absolute difference between entry and stop                    |
| Target              | Nearest valid opposing marked liquidity level                 |
| Minimum reward/risk | Target distance must be at least 1.5 times initial risk       |
| Time stop           | Exit after 60 minutes                                         |
| End-of-day stop     | Exit no later than 3:15 PM IST                                |
| Daily limit         | Maximum one trade; after a completed entry, no second attempt |

Here, \\(A\\) is the ATR measured at the sweep confirmation and frozen for that trade's initial stop calculation.

Reject the trade if:

- The stop is on the wrong side of the entry or is impractically wide.
- The nearest opposing liquidity level offers less than 1.5R.
- The entry is outside 9:30–11:00 AM IST.
- The 1-minute structure break occurred before the required reclaim confirmation.
- The price has already travelled so far that the next executable entry no longer satisfies the 1.5R condition.
- The daily trade limit has already been used.

For the initial index-chart test, record SENSEX points and R-multiples. If you later trade options, option premium movement, bid–ask spread, slippage and charges must be tested separately; an index signal's theoretical result is not the same as an option trade's actual result.

## 3. Early-warning layer — what to watch before the entry

The detector should provide three distinct statuses, so you can prepare early without confusing a warning with a trade signal.

Stage 1 — WATCH

Price approaches a marked level within \\(0.15A\\). Start observing the 1-minute candles. No trade yet.

Stage 2 — SWEEP CONFIRMED

Price penetrates the level by at least \\(0.05A\\), and the 5-minute candle closes back inside. Record the sweep extreme and begin looking for the micro-structure break.

Stage 3 — ENTRY ELIGIBLE

The 1-minute structure break closes in the reversal direction, and stop, target, timing and reward/risk checks all pass.

The proximity threshold and minimum sweep penetration are experimental settings. Record their results before tuning them. Do not move thresholds around during a test to make past setups look better.

### Phase 1 — lock the rules

- Save the rules above as `SwingPilot Early Sweep Detector V1.0`.
- Use 5-minute and 1-minute candlesticks consistently.
- Keep the same ATR period and thresholds throughout the initial test.
- Write down each day's marked levels before examining the subsequent setup.
- Record every qualifying sweep, including ones that do not become trades.

### Phase 2 — historical chart replay

Start with 30 trading sessions, then extend the sample if there are too few qualifying signals.

For each session:

1. Open the SENSEX 5-minute chart and mark previous-day high/low and the 9:15–9:30 opening range.
2. Inspect the session from 9:30 to 11:00 AM.
3. Record each level penetration and whether the 5-minute candle reclaimed the level.
4. Switch to the 1-minute chart and identify the first qualifying structure break.
5. Record the entry price that would actually have been available after the signal—not an idealized price from an earlier candle.
6. Apply the stop, target and time-exit rules.
7. Continue tracking the trade outcome until exit, even if the target was not reached.

Use screenshots with timestamps for ambiguous cases. If the chart does not let you establish the candle sequence confidently, mark the case uncertain instead of forcing it into a win or loss.

### Phase 4 — evaluate performance

Do not judge the strategy by win rate alone. Calculate:

| Metric                        | What it tells you                                              |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Qualifying setups per session | Whether opportunities are frequent enough                      |
| Entry delay                   | Time between reclaim confirmation and executable entry         |
| Win rate                      | Percentage of completed trades with positive net results       |
| Average win / average loss    | Size of wins compared with losses                              |
| Expectancy in R               | Average risk-adjusted result per trade                         |
| Maximum drawdown              | Depth of the worst equity decline                              |
| Missed reversals              | Sweeps that reversed but did not satisfy entry rules           |
| False sweeps                  | Qualifying sweeps that continued against the intended reversal |
| Costs and slippage            | Whether a theoretical edge survives execution costs            |

Use the following expectancy calculation:

\\[ E_R=(P_w\times \bar W_R)-(P_l\times \bar L_R) \\]

Here, \\(P_w\\) and \\(P_l\\) are the win and loss proportions, while \\(\bar W_R\\) and \\(\bar L_R\\) are average win and loss measured in units of initial risk. Include costs in the trade results.

A positive result over 30 sessions is not sufficient proof of an edge. Treat 30 sessions as an initial screening sample, then test a separate, untouched period. Do not optimize the thresholds repeatedly against the same sample.

## 5. Decision gates for moving beyond the prototype

- Gate 1 — rule consistency: two reviews of the same chart should produce the same sweep, reclaim and structure-break decision.
- Gate 2 — signal quality: measure how often qualifying entries reach the planned target before the stop.
- Gate 3 — execution realism: apply realistic entry slippage, exits and costs.
- Gate 4 — out-of-sample validation: test on later sessions that were not used to choose parameters.
- Gate 5 — paper trading: observe live signals without placing real orders before considering any live deployment.

These are validation gates, not promises of profitability.

## My recommendation

Keep the balanced entry exactly as defined for V1: 5-minute sweep and reclaim, then a confirmed 1-minute micro-structure break, then entry if the risk checks pass. Do not add volume filters, RSI, VWAP or other indicators yet; they would make it harder to tell which part of the detector is helping.

The first deliverable should be a reliable manual dataset of SENSEX setups from Kite charts. Once we have those results, we can decide whether the early warning is useful, whether the structure confirmation is too slow, and whether a retest rule improves results enough to justify missed entries.
