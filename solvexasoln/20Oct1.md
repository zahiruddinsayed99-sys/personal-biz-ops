# Review & Analysis: Rajesh Jain’s 9:15 AM Options Buying Strategy

Based on your summary of the video, Rajesh Jain’s approach is an opening-session momentum strategy. The idea is to identify stocks showing strong directional movement immediately after the NSE market opens, buy an ATM option in the same direction, and exit quickly rather than holding a trade for the entire session.

My assessment: the concept is worth testing, but the claim of a high-probability trade is not established by the strategy description alone. The opening session offers opportunity, but it also brings sharp reversals, bid–ask spreads, option-premium volatility and false breakouts.

## 1. The strategy summarized

Timing: 9:15–9:30 AM

Observe the opening move, look for a directional opportunity and aim for a quick exit. The 9:30 AM exit is a target, not a guarantee that a profitable trade will be available.

Stock selection: NSE F&O gainers and losers

Identify stocks with unusually strong early price movement. A stock appearing on the top-gainers list is not automatically a buy; the opening move must be confirmed by price action.

Entry: opening-level breakout

Observe the initial price action for around 30 seconds, then look for a break of the opening reference level. A sustained breakout is more meaningful than a brief spike.

Instrument: ATM options

Buy an ATM Call when the underlying stock confirms bullish momentum, or an ATM Put when it confirms bearish momentum. Liquidity and execution costs matter.

Discipline: “Good Night” rule

Stop trading after reaching the predefined profit objective—or the daily loss limit. Do not force a trade merely because the market is open.

## 2. What is good about this strategy?

| Strength                     | Why it matters                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Focus on the opening session | Overnight news and fresh order flow can create meaningful price movement.                                                             |
| Individual stock selection   | Some stocks move much more decisively than the broader index.                                                                         |
| Simple breakout concept      | Opening levels provide a reference for objective entry rules.                                                                         |
| ATM options                  | Generally offer a more direct response to underlying price changes than far-OTM options, although liquidity and pricing still matter. |
| Short holding period         | Limits exposure to prolonged intraday uncertainty if exits are executed as planned.                                                   |
| Strict stop-trading rule     | Reduces the temptation to give back profits through repeated trades.                                                                  |

The most valuable element is not the 9:15 AM timing by itself. It is the combination of stock selection, confirmed momentum, controlled risk and disciplined execution.

## 3. Where the strategy can fail

This is the most important part of the review. Several details in the video summary need more precise rules before the strategy is ready for live trading.

Risk 1 — The first 30 seconds can be misleading

Opening orders can cause sharp price spikes in either direction. A stock breaking its opening price does not necessarily mean the next 15 minutes will continue in that direction.

Improvement: Define an opening range and require a candle close beyond the range, rather than entering on a momentary breach.

Risk 2 — Top gainers and losers can reverse

A stock may be a top gainer because of an overnight gap, but its early buyers may sell into that strength. The same applies to top losers.

Improvement: Check the gap, current volume, opening-range structure and whether the stock is holding its breakout.

Risk 3 — ATM options are not automatically safe

ATM options typically have meaningful directional sensitivity, but their premium can fall even when the stock moves slightly in your favour. The bid–ask spread, implied volatility and rapid premium fluctuations matter.

Improvement: Check option liquidity, spread and premium movement before entering. Use a predefined exit rather than relying on the stock direction alone.

Risk 4 — A fixed 9:30 AM exit can be too rigid

Exiting by 9:30 AM limits holding time, but it does not guarantee a profit. A trade can hit its stop before 9:30, or a valid setup may occur later.

Improvement: Use a hard risk-based exit first and a time-based exit second. Do not stay in a losing trade just because the planned exit time has not arrived.

One additional caution: the explanation that FII and DII activity drives market movement is an oversimplification. Opening prices reflect many participants, overnight information, global cues, liquidity and order imbalances. A retail trader generally cannot infer institutional buying or selling from price movement alone.

## 4. A more mechanical version of the strategy

I would convert the idea into the following testable rules. These are my proposed refinements, not verified rules from Rajesh Jain's original method.

## Opening Momentum — Proposed Rulebook

Illustrative framework for paper trading on liquid NSE F&O stocks.

1. 9:15–9:16 — Build the opening range

   Record the first one-minute candle's high, low, open and close. Avoid treating the very first tick as a reliable trend.
2. 9:16 onward — Select the candidate

   Screen F&O stocks among the early gainers and losers. Prefer stocks with sufficient liquidity, meaningful volume and a clear directional move.
3. Confirm the underlying breakout
   - Bullish: price closes above the opening-range high.
   - Bearish: price closes below the opening-range low.
   - No confirmed breakout: no trade.
4. Check the quality of the move

   Look for sustained price movement, stronger-than-usual volume and room to the next nearby swing high or swing low. Reject a breakout that runs directly into a major obstacle.
5. Select the option
   - Bullish breakout → liquid ATM Call.
   - Bearish breakout → liquid ATM Put.
   Verify the bid–ask spread and available liquidity before placing an order.
6. Set the risk before entry

   Define the maximum rupee loss, option-premium stop and profit-taking rule. Calculate position size from the planned loss, including estimated charges and slippage.
7. Exit and stop trading

   Exit on the stop, the planned target, a confirmed failure of the breakout, or the strategy's time cutoff—whichever applicable rule comes first. Stop after the daily loss limit or profit objective is reached.

### Example of a mechanical entry

Suppose a stock's first one-minute candle has the following prices:

## 6. How to validate whether it actually works

Before risking real money, test at least 50–100 eligible historical or paper-traded setups, ideally across different market conditions. A larger sample will give a more reliable estimate.

Record:

- Stock and date
- Opening-range high and low
- Breakout direction and entry time
- Underlying entry price
- ATM option strike, premium and spread
- Entry and exit prices
- Maximum favourable and adverse movement
- Gross and net profit/loss after costs
- Whether each decision gate passed

Evaluate the results using:

\\[ \text{Expectancy} = (P_w \times W) - (P_l \times L) \\]

Here, \\(P_w\\) is the win rate, \\(W\\) is average winning trade, \\(P_l\\) is the loss rate, and \\(L\\) is average losing trade. Use net outcomes after costs for the final assessment.

For example, a hypothetical strategy with a 55% win rate, average winning trade of ₹500 and average losing trade of ₹700 has an expectancy of:

\\[ 0.55(500)-0.45(700)=-₹40 \\]

So, even a strategy that wins more often than it loses can be unprofitable.

The core idea is reasonable: trade a confirmed early directional move instead of predicting the market. The part that needs proof is whether the combination of stock selection, breakout confirmation and ATM option execution produces a positive expectancy after transaction costs.

For your trading playbook, I would prioritise the opening-range definition, mandatory decision gates, stop-loss calculation and no-trade rules before considering this strategy ready for live trading.
